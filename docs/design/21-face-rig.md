# 21 — Face Rig: Bone-Driven Face Sculpting

Status: **blueprint** (2026-11-09). It replaces the face half of D69 (morph-target face sliders, [20 §5](20-champion-creator.md#5-face)) with a bone-driven face, **D76**. Free sculpting (CR9, [20 §5.3](20-champion-creator.md#53-free-sculpting)) stays, carried over to the new head. §9 extends the same system to the body.

Why: the owner's verdict on the slider face (2026-11-09) is that it looks amateurish, and the sliders don't read as the structures they move. The target is the click-and-drag face editors of Fallout 4 and Black Desert Online. You point at a feature, it lights up, and you drag it. The only part of today's editor to keep is the sculpting.

The topology reference is `fb/face-topo-ref.jpg` (owner-supplied). It shows concentric loops round the eyes and the mouth, a nasolabial loop from the nose's wing past the mouth corner, a jawline loop, a modelled nose with alar creases, and a grid that relaxes over the cranium and down into the neck.

---

## 0. The design in one paragraph

Every humanoid base shares **one authored head mesh** (~1,100 vertices) skinned to **24 face bones** under `head`, at most four influences per vertex.

- **A face is 24 small transform deltas:** translation, rotation and scale per bone relative to its rest, quantized, at most ~0.5 KB.
- **The forge bakes them into the face bones' rest transforms,** and the client builds the skin from the base's **inverse bind matrices**. Skinning on the GPU does the rest: the vertex buffer is never rebuilt for a face, animation is untouched (clips never key face bones), and a crowd of sculpted champions costs nothing beyond ordinary skinning.
- **In the creator,** a Rust node (`CharacterSculptController`) maps a mouse ray to a face **region** and highlights it through a shader uniform. A drag becomes a camera-plane displacement, written straight to that region's bones on the `Skeleton3D`. No forge, no mesh upload, while dragging.
- **Free sculpting** stays as sparse vertex offsets in bind space, applied once at forge time underneath the bones.

## 1. Topology and rig

### 1.1 Who makes the head

The head is **authored** in Blender (`art/creator/heads/head_base.blend`), not generated by code. Code-made faces have been rejected twice as programmer-made (2026-11-07, 2026-11-09).

- **One topology for every humanoid base.** Slim and heavy, masculine and feminine share the same vertex order, so face data, regions and weights mean the same thing on all four. Their differences are four **shape variants** of that one mesh, baked into each base at export.
- **Retopology from a sculpt.** The artist sculpts a neutral head at high resolution, then retopologizes to the loop specification below, using the reference as the guide.
  - A script may lay out a starting cage (the loop counts in §1.2) to save time.
  - The shape is the artist's.
  - When Claude does this work through the Blender MCP, it shows the owner a front, ¾ and profile capture of the wireframe and the shaded head before anything ships.

### 1.2 Edge-loop specification (~1,100 vertices)

All quads, except **poles** (3- and 5-valence vertices). Poles go only where the table puts them, never on a fold line or a lip or lid edge.

|---|---|---|

| **Orbicularis oris** | 6 concentric loops round the mouth, 20 vertices on the innermost (the lip line; the mouth is closed, so the line is a crease, not a hole). | **Brow** | 2 horizontal loops over each orbit (the brow ridge), joined by the glabella column. | 60 |
| **Cheeks and zygoma** | A grid filling between the eye, nasolabial and jaw loops: 6 × 6 per side, the zygomatic arch along one row. | 2 × 50 |
| **Jawline** | One continuous loop from each ear lobe along the mandible to the chin. It separates face from neck. The gonial angle is a vertex on it (the jaw-angle bone's pivot). | 40 |

Total, about **1,100 unique positions**. After splits for color and UV seams that is ~1,350 vertices and ~2,150 triangles, plus ~150 for the eyes.

Loops must be **continuous and flow along the muscles**: what deforms together sits on one loop. Polygon sizes even out from dense (lids, lips, alae) to sparse (cranium, back of neck) over at least two loops, so there are no pinched transitions.

### 1.3 The bone grid: 24 face bones

Every face bone is a child of `head` (or of `jaw` or `nose_bridge` where noted). Every one carries a rest transform at its pivot, oriented so its +Y points out of the face along the surface normal at the pivot and its +Z points up (Blender roll 0). A delta's `t: (x, y, z)` therefore means sideways, out of the face and up, in the bone's own frame. The `_l` bones are on +X, and `_r` mirrors them (10 §2).

| # | Bone | Parent | Pivot | What it moves | DOFs (T / R / S) |

| 5, 6 | `face_eye_l` / `_r` | `head` | eye centre | the whole orbit: spacing, height, depth, tilt, size; the eyeball is its child | T, R, S |
| 7, 8 | `face_cheekbone_l` / `_r` | `head` | zygoma | cheekbone height, width and prominence | T, R, S |

**Scale is local and does not cascade.** A scaled parent would skew its children (Godot's skeleton has no per-bone "inherit scale" switch). The deltas therefore obey two rules:

- **Leaves may scale freely.**
- **The parents `jaw`, `face_nose_bridge` and `face_eye_*`** apply their delta scale with the **inverse scale composed onto their children's deltas** (§2.3). In the data a parent's scale means "this region's size", never "everything under me grows".

### 1.4 Weights: no pinching

- **Four influences at most** per vertex (glTF, `mftr-pack`), normalized, quantized to 1/255 by the exporter.
- **Every head vertex has `head` weight;** face bones take only what their region claims. A vertex outside every face region is 100% `head`, so the cranium and back of the neck never move with the face.
- **Regions are painted along loops, not by distance in space.** Each face bone has a **core** (weight 1.0, the loops listed in §1.3) and a **falloff band** of 2–3 loops in which its weight eases from 1 to 0 with a smoothstep along the surface (geodesic distance on the mesh, not euclidean, so the upper lip never drags the nose). The exporter computes the falloff from the authored cores, which the artist paints as vertex groups `core.<bone>`.
- **Neighbours overlap by at least one loop,** and their weights cross smoothly there. Along any edge, a single bone's weight may change by **at most 0.35** (the validator checks this): bigger jumps are what pinch.
- **Lids and lips are split, not blended,** across their edge line:
  - The upper lid row belongs to the eye bone.
  - The upper lip row belongs to `face_lip_upper`, the lower to `face_lip_lower` (through `jaw`).
  - The lip-line vertices are shared and weighted 50/50 between the lip bones, so a closed mouth stays closed under any sculpt.
  - The mouth corners carry `face_mouth_*` at 0.6, blended to both lips.
- **The eye bones carry their orbit rigidly** for the inner 2 loops (the lid edge and lid fold) and fall off over loops 3–5. A sculpted eye moves as a unit and never tears the lid from the eyeball.

### 1.5 The neck seam

The head mesh joins the body at a **locked boundary ring** of 24 vertices.

- **The same positions as the body's neck ring.** The body's ring is part of the same base, and the exporter welds the two (one vertex each), so there is no crack under any pose.
- **The ring's weights equal the body ring's** (`neck`/`head` blend), and **no face bone may weigh on the ring or on the two loops above it.** The validator checks both. Face sculpts therefore never move the seam.
- **Normals on the ring** are averaged across head and body at export (split normals), so the seam doesn't shade as a line.
- **Free sculpting's envelope is zero on the ring** and eases to full over two loops above it (the same rule as the bones).
- **Body sliders and proportions** that move the neck move the ring through the body's own weights. The head follows through `head`, so the seam stays welded.

### 2.2 Data: bone driver offsets

```rust
// crates/mftr-forge/src/face.rs
//! Bone-driven faces (21): a face is a small transform delta per face bone, relative to the
//! bone's rest, quantized so a recipe stays small and every client bakes the same face.

/// The face rig's bones, in rig order (21 §1.3). Index = position in every table below.
pub const FACE_BONES: [&str; 24] = [
    "face_forehead", "face_brow_mid", "face_brow_l", "face_brow_r", "face_eye_l", "face_eye_r",
    "face_cheekbone_l", "face_cheekbone_r", "face_cheek_l", "face_cheek_r", "face_nose_bridge",
    "face_nose_tip", "face_nostril_l", "face_nostril_r", "face_lip_upper", "jaw", "face_lip_lower",
    "face_mouth_l", "face_mouth_r", "face_chin", "face_jaw_angle_l", "face_jaw_angle_r",
    "face_ear_l", "face_ear_r",
];
pub const RIG_VERSION: u8 = 1;

/// Fixed-point units: translation 0.1 mm, rotation 0.01° (a rotation vector), scale 0.1%
/// in log space (so 2× and ½× are symmetric).
pub const T_UNIT: f32 = 1.0e-4;
pub const R_UNIT: f32 = 0.01;
pub const S_UNIT: f32 = 1.0e-3;

/// One bone's delta from rest, in the bone's own rest frame.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, Default, PartialEq, Eq, Hash)]
pub struct BoneDelta {
    pub t: [i16; 3],
    pub r: [i16; 3],
    pub s: [i16; 3],
}

impl BoneDelta {
    pub const ZERO: BoneDelta = BoneDelta { t: [0; 3], r: [0; 3], s: [0; 3] };

    pub fn is_zero(&self) -> bool {
        *self == Self::ZERO
    }

    pub fn translation(&self) -> [f32; 3] {
        self.t.map(|v| v as f32 * T_UNIT)
    }

    /// Rotation as a quaternion (x, y, z, w) from the rotation vector.
    pub fn rotation(&self) -> [f32; 4] {
        let v = self.r.map(|d| (d as f32 * R_UNIT).to_radians());
        let angle = (v[0] * v[0] + v[1] * v[1] + v[2] * v[2]).sqrt();
        if angle < 1e-7 {
            return [0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0];
        }
        let (s, c) = (angle * 0.5).sin_cos();
        let k = s / angle;
        [v[0] * k, v[1] * k, v[2] * k, c]
    }

    pub fn scale(&self) -> [f32; 3] {
        self.s.map(|v| (v as f32 * S_UNIT).exp2())
    }

    /// The left/right mirror (X flipped): translation x, rotation y and z change sign.
    pub fn mirrored(&self) -> BoneDelta {
        BoneDelta { t: [-self.t[0], self.t[1], self.t[2]], r: [self.r[0], -self.r[1], -self.r[2]], s: self.s }
    }
}

/// How far each bone may go (the envelope, 20 §6.2: silhouettes and hitboxes stay honest).
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug)]
pub struct Limits {
    pub t: [i16; 3],
    pub r: [i16; 3],
    pub s: [i16; 3],
    /// Which of T, R, S this bone takes (§1.3's DOF column).
    pub dofs: u8,
}

pub const DOF_T: u8 = 1;
pub const DOF_R: u8 = 2;
pub const DOF_S: u8 = 4;

/// A face: one delta per face bone.
#[derive(Clone, Debug, Default, PartialEq, Eq, Hash)]
pub struct FaceSculpt {
    pub rig: u8,
    pub bones: [BoneDelta; 24],
}

impl FaceSculpt {
    /// Each delta brought inside its bone's limits and DOFs.
    pub fn clamped(&self, limits: &[Limits; 24]) -> FaceSculpt {
        let mut out = self.clone();
        for (d, l) in out.bones.iter_mut().zip(limits) {
            let c = |v: [i16; 3], m: [i16; 3], on: bool| -> [i16; 3] {
                if !on { [0; 3] } else { [0, 1, 2].map(|k| v[k].clamp(-m[k], m[k])) }
            };
            d.t = c(d.t, l.t, l.dofs & DOF_T != 0);
            d.r = c(d.r, l.r, l.dofs & DOF_R != 0);
            d.s = c(d.s, l.s, l.dofs & DOF_S != 0);
        }
        out
    }
}
```

Starting limits *(start, tuned on the authored head)*:

| Bone kind | Translation | Rotation | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ears, nose tip, chin | ±40 mm | ±30° | 0.6–1.8× |
| Other features | ±25 mm | ±20° | 0.7–1.5× |
| Jaw and forehead | ±20 mm | ±12° | 0.8–1.3× |

These are bounded like `sculpt::MAX_OFFSET`. Free sculpting remains the way past them, inside its own envelope.

### 2.3 The bake: deltas into rest transforms

```rust
/// Each face bone's parent among the face bones (scale compensation, 21 §1.3).
pub const FACE_PARENT: [Option<usize>; 24] = {
    let mut p = [None; 24];
    p[11] = Some(10); // face_nose_tip under face_nose_bridge
    p[12] = Some(11); // face_nostril_l under face_nose_tip
    p[13] = Some(11); // face_nostril_r
    p[16] = Some(15); // face_lip_lower under jaw
    p[19] = Some(15); // face_chin under jaw
    p[20] = Some(15); // face_jaw_angle_l under jaw
    p[21] = Some(15); // face_jaw_angle_r
    p
};

/// Bake `face` into a forged model: each face bone's rest becomes rest · delta, and a face
/// bone whose parent was scaled has that scale divided back out (scale stays local, §1.3).
/// The skin keeps the base's inverse bind matrices, so the mesh deforms by exactly the deltas.
/// Parents precede children in `FACE_BONES`, so one pass suffices.
pub fn bake(model: &mut mftr_pack::glb::Model, face: &FaceSculpt) {
    let index: Vec<Option<usize>> = FACE_BONES.iter().map(|n| model.node_by_name(n)).collect();
    for (i, d) in face.bones.iter().enumerate() {
        let Some(node) = index[i] else { continue };
        let n = &mut model.nodes[node];
        if let Some(p) = FACE_PARENT[i] {
            // Cancel the parent's delta scale (exact for uniform scale; for non-uniform
            // scale it's exact along the parent's axes, which the limits keep near-uniform).
            let ps = face.bones[p].scale();
            let inv = [1.0 / ps[0], 1.0 / ps[1], 1.0 / ps[2]];
            n.translation = mul(n.translation, inv);
            n.scale = mul(n.scale, inv);
        }
        if d.is_zero() {
            continue;
        }
        // Translation is in the bone's rest frame: rotate it into the parent's.
        n.translation = add(n.translation, qrot(n.rotation, d.translation()));
        n.rotation = qmul(n.rotation, d.rotation());
        n.scale = mul(n.scale, d.scale());
    }
}
```

The forge calls `bake` after proportions and before parts are attached. Its rules:

- **Parts follow by weights.** Head parts (hair, hats, beards, helmets, masks) get the head mesh's weights by nearest-surface transfer at base export. The face-slider shape keys on parts go away.
- **Free sculpting (`sculpt.rs`) runs before `bake`** on bind-space positions, so bones carry sculpted vertices like any others.
- **Determinism:** `bake` uses only `f32` arithmetic on fixed-point input, the same on every client (D19). A face is cosmetic and never enters the sim.

**The one client change this needs:** `MftrModel::instantiate` must build the `Skin` from the glTF **inverse bind matrices**, not from `create_skin_from_rest_transforms()`. Built from rest, the skin would cancel the baked deltas. `mftr_pack::glb` already parses skins, and it needs to keep the IBM accessor.

### 2.4 Region map: raycast hit → region → bones

```rust
/// A face region (21 §3.2): what lights up under the cursor and which bones a drag moves.
#[derive(Clone, Debug)]
pub struct Region {
    pub id: u8,
    pub name: &'static str,
    /// Bone indices into FACE_BONES with their share of the drag (1.0 primary; < 1.0 a soft
    /// neighbour, e.g. dragging the nose tip moves the bridge 0.25).
    pub bones: Vec<(u8, f32)>,
    /// The region on the other side (mirror editing), or itself on the middle line.
    pub mirror: u8,
    /// What a plain drag does here: the DOF a left-drag edits.
    pub primary: u8,
}

/// The base's region table and each head vertex's region (exported with the base).
pub struct RegionTable {
    pub regions: Vec<Region>,
    /// Per head-mesh vertex (in the forged mesh's order): its region id, 255 = none (cranium,
    /// neck, ring: not editable).
    pub vertex_region: Vec<u8>,
}

impl RegionTable {
    /// The region a ray hits on the posed head: the region most of the hit triangle's
    /// vertices share (ties go to the nearest vertex).
    pub fn region_of_triangle(&self, tri: [u32; 3], bary: [f32; 3]) -> Option<u8> {
        let ids = tri.map(|v| self.vertex_region[v as usize]);
        let best = if ids[0] == ids[1] || ids[0] == ids[2] {
            ids[0]
        } else if ids[1] == ids[2] {
            ids[1]
        } else {
            let k = (0..3).max_by(|&a, &b| bary[a].total_cmp(&bary[b])).unwrap_or(0);
            ids[k]
        };
        (best != 255).then_some(best)
    }
}
```

The regions *(start)* are the bones' cores merged into what a player names:

| Feature | Regions |
|---|---|
| Brow | forehead, brow (L/R), glabella |
| Eyes | eye (L/R) |
| Cheeks | cheekbone (L/R), cheek (L/R) |
| Nose | bridge, nose tip, nostril (L/R) |
| Mouth | upper lip, lower lip, mouth corner (L/R) |
| Jaw | chin, jaw (the whole lower face), jaw angle (L/R) |
| Ears | ear (L/R) |

That makes 21 regions. Blender writes them per vertex from the `core.<bone>` groups (the vertex's strongest core). The exporter carries them in the base as a vertex attribute (§3.2) and in `<base>.face.ron`.

### 2.5 Screen drag → bone delta

```rust
/// What the creator's camera knows, enough to turn pixels into metres.
pub struct CameraFrame {
    pub origin: [f32; 3],
    pub right: [f32; 3],
    pub up: [f32; 3],
    pub forward: [f32; 3],
    /// Vertical field of view in radians (perspective), or 0 for orthographic.
    pub fov_y: f32,
    /// Orthographic size (metres, viewport height) when `fov_y` is 0.
    pub ortho_size: f32,
    pub viewport_h: f32,
}

/// The world displacement under a mouse drag of `drag_px` (x right, y down), on the plane
/// through `anchor` facing the camera: the feature moves exactly with the cursor.
pub fn drag_to_world(cam: &CameraFrame, drag_px: [f32; 2], anchor: [f32; 3]) -> [f32; 3] {
    let per_px = if cam.fov_y > 0.0 {
        let depth = dot(sub(anchor, cam.origin), cam.forward).max(0.05);
        2.0 * depth * (cam.fov_y * 0.5).tan() / cam.viewport_h
    } else {
        cam.ortho_size / cam.viewport_h
    };
    add(scale(cam.right, drag_px[0] * per_px), scale(cam.up, -drag_px[1] * per_px))
}

/// What a drag edits.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum DragMode {
    /// Left drag: move the feature in the camera plane.
    Move,
    /// Right drag: turn it (horizontal drag about the camera's up, vertical about its right).
    Turn,
    /// Wheel or Shift-drag: grow or shrink it (vertical drag; Alt for width only).
    Size { width_only: bool },
}

/// Apply a drag to one bone's delta. `bone_rest_world` is the bone's rest basis in world space
/// (its frame for the delta) with the bone's global position; `share` the region's share for
/// this bone. Returns the new delta, clamped.
pub fn apply_drag(
    delta: BoneDelta,
    mode: DragMode,
    world: [f32; 3],
    drag_px: [f32; 2],
    cam: &CameraFrame,
    bone_rest_world: &[[f32; 3]; 3],
    share: f32,
    limits: &Limits,
) -> BoneDelta {
    let to_local = |v: [f32; 3]| -> [f32; 3] {
        // Columns of the basis are the bone's axes in world; project onto them (orthonormal rest).
        [dot(v, bone_rest_world[0]), dot(v, bone_rest_world[1]), dot(v, bone_rest_world[2])]
    };
    let mut d = delta;
    match mode {
        DragMode::Move => {
            let local = to_local(scale(world, share));
            for k in 0..3 {
                d.t[k] = d.t[k].saturating_add((local[k] / T_UNIT).round() as i16);
            }
        }
        DragMode::Turn => {
            // 0.4° a pixel, about the camera's up (horizontal drag) and right (vertical drag).
            let deg = 0.4 * share;
            let axis_world = add(scale(cam.up, drag_px[0] * deg), scale(cam.right, drag_px[1] * deg));
            let local = to_local(axis_world);
            for k in 0..3 {
                d.r[k] = d.r[k].saturating_add((local[k] / R_UNIT).round() as i16);
            }
        }
        DragMode::Size { width_only } => {
            // 0.5% a pixel up, as log2 steps.
            let step = (-drag_px[1] * 0.005 * share / S_UNIT).round() as i16;
            if width_only {
                d.s[0] = d.s[0].saturating_add(step);
            } else {
                for k in 0..3 {
                    d.s[k] = d.s[k].saturating_add(step);
                }
            }
        }
    }
    clamp_one(d, limits)
}

/// One delta inside its bone's limits and DOFs.
pub fn clamp_one(d: BoneDelta, l: &Limits) -> BoneDelta {
    let c = |v: [i16; 3], m: [i16; 3], on: bool| if on { [0, 1, 2].map(|k| v[k].clamp(-m[k], m[k])) } else { [0; 3] };
    BoneDelta { t: c(d.t, l.t, l.dofs & DOF_T != 0), r: c(d.r, l.r, l.dofs & DOF_R != 0), s: c(d.s, l.s, l.dofs & DOF_S != 0) }
}
```

**Mirroring:** with symmetry on (the default, as for sculpting), the same drag goes to the mirror region's bones through `BoneDelta::mirrored` on the X-flipped world vector. Middle-line regions (nose, lips, chin, forehead) lock their X translation to zero while symmetry is on.

### 2.6 Serialization

**In the recipe (canonical, RON, D68):** only the bones that moved, in fixed-point units:

```ron
looks: (
    build: "humanoid_heavy",
    face: (rig: 1, bones: (
        face_nose_tip: (t: (0, -120, -40), s: (80, 80, 80)),
        face_brow_l: (t: (0, 0, -35), r: (0, 0, 600)),
        face_brow_r: (t: (0, 0, -35), r: (0, 0, -600)),
        face_chin: (t: (0, -90, 0)),
    )),
    sculpt: ( ... ),   // unchanged: free sculpting, by weld
)
```

Absent fields are zero, so a face is 0–2 KB of text. The reader is strict: it rejects unknown bones, out-of-range values (silently clamping would let two clients disagree), and a `rig` the build doesn't know. Recipes stay text over the wire, as today.

**Compact binary (sync, hashing, the live editor's undo stack):**

```rust
/// `FS` + version + rig, a 32-bit mask of the bones present, then per present bone a 9-bit
/// mask of the components present and those components as little-endian i16. At most
/// 4 + 4 + 24 × (2 + 18) = 488 bytes; a typical face ~60 bytes.
pub fn to_bytes(face: &FaceSculpt) -> Vec<u8> {
    let mut out = vec![b'F', b'S', 1, face.rig];
    let mut mask = 0u32;
    for (i, b) in face.bones.iter().enumerate() {
        if !b.is_zero() {
            mask |= 1 << i;
        }
    }
    out.extend_from_slice(&mask.to_le_bytes());
    for b in face.bones.iter().filter(|b| !b.is_zero()) {
        let comps: [i16; 9] = [b.t[0], b.t[1], b.t[2], b.r[0], b.r[1], b.r[2], b.s[0], b.s[1], b.s[2]];
        let mut cmask = 0u16;
        for (k, v) in comps.iter().enumerate() {
            if *v != 0 {
                cmask |= 1 << k;
            }
        }
        out.extend_from_slice(&cmask.to_le_bytes());
        for v in comps.iter().filter(|v| **v != 0) {
            out.extend_from_slice(&v.to_le_bytes());
        }
    }
    out
}

pub fn from_bytes(bytes: &[u8]) -> Result<FaceSculpt, String> {
    let get = |at: usize, n: usize| bytes.get(at..at + n).ok_or("face: truncated");
    if get(0, 3)? != [b'F', b'S', 1] {
        return Err("face: not a version-1 face".into());
    }
    let rig = bytes[3];
    let mask = u32::from_le_bytes(get(4, 4)?.try_into().unwrap());
    if mask >> FACE_BONES.len() != 0 {
        return Err("face: unknown bones".into());
    }
    let mut face = FaceSculpt { rig, bones: [BoneDelta::ZERO; 24] };
    let mut at = 8;
    for i in 0..FACE_BONES.len() {
        if mask & (1 << i) == 0 {
            continue;
        }
        let cmask = u16::from_le_bytes(get(at, 2)?.try_into().unwrap());
        at += 2;
        if cmask >> 9 != 0 {
            return Err("face: bad component mask".into());
        }
        let mut comps = [0i16; 9];
        for (k, c) in comps.iter_mut().enumerate() {
            if cmask & (1 << k) != 0 {
                *c = i16::from_le_bytes(get(at, 2)?.try_into().unwrap());
                at += 2;
            }
        }
        face.bones[i] = BoneDelta { t: [comps[0], comps[1], comps[2]], r: [comps[3], comps[4], comps[5]], s: [comps[6], comps[7], comps[8]] };
    }
    if at != bytes.len() {
        return Err("face: trailing bytes".into());
    }
    Ok(face)
}
```

Round-trip tests come with it: random faces → bytes → face; RON → face → RON; a mirrored face re-mirrors to itself.

### 2.7 Migration from the slider face

Each existing face slider becomes a **preset**: a `FaceSculpt` at +1, mirrored where it is two-sided. A recipe's `shape: (nose_reach: 0.6, …)` face entries load as the weighted sum of their presets, clamped. Body sliders are untouched until §9.

`sculpt:` offsets on the old head (by weld id) can't map 1:1 onto a new topology, so they are dropped from the head on load. The rest of the body keeps its welds. The loader reports this, and the official recipes are re-made (they carry few face sculpts).

### 3.1 The input pipeline (creator.gd, the Face step)

```gdscript
# creator.gd: the Face step's viewport input. `_sculptor` is the CharacterSculptController
# (Rust, §4) for the preview's Skeleton3D; `_camera` the stage camera.
var _sculptor: CharacterSculptController
var _dragging := false

func _face_input(event: InputEvent) -> void:
	if event is InputEventMouseMotion:
		var origin := _camera.project_ray_origin(event.position)
		var dir := _camera.project_ray_normal(event.position)
		if _dragging:
			_sculptor.drag(event.relative, _camera_frame())
		else:
			var region := _sculptor.hover(origin, dir)
			_material.set_shader_parameter("hover_region", region)
	elif event is InputEventMouseButton:
		if event.pressed and event.button_index in [MOUSE_BUTTON_LEFT, MOUSE_BUTTON_RIGHT]:
			var mode := "move" if event.button_index == MOUSE_BUTTON_LEFT else "turn"
			if event.shift_pressed:
				mode = "size_width" if event.alt_pressed else "size"
			_dragging = _sculptor.begin_drag(mode, _symmetry)
			if _dragging:
				_material.set_shader_parameter("active_region", _sculptor.active_region())
		elif not event.pressed and _dragging:
			_dragging = false
			# One undo step per drag; the recipe takes the face (no forge needed: parts follow
			# the bones by their weights).
			creator.set_face(_sculptor.end_drag())
			_material.set_shader_parameter("active_region", -1)
		elif event.pressed and event.button_index in [MOUSE_BUTTON_WHEEL_UP, MOUSE_BUTTON_WHEEL_DOWN]:
			var step := 12.0 if event.button_index == MOUSE_BUTTON_WHEEL_UP else -12.0
			_sculptor.nudge_size(step, _symmetry)
			creator.set_face(_sculptor.face_bytes())

func _camera_frame() -> Dictionary:
	var t := _camera.global_transform
	return {"origin": t.origin, "right": t.basis.x, "up": t.basis.y, "forward": -t.basis.z,
		"fov_y": deg_to_rad(_camera.fov), "viewport_h": float(_viewport.size.y)}
```

The free-sculpt brushes keep their toolbar on the same step. With a brush selected, the drag sculpts (as today). With **Features** selected (the new default), it drives bones. The two compose, since sculpting moves bind-space vertices and bones carry them.

### 3.2 Gizmo-less highlighting

No gizmos, handles or outlines are drawn. The skin under the cursor brightens, and the region being dragged glows in the signature hue. The per-vertex region id rides in the mesh as `CUSTOM0` (set once, when the preview mesh is built), and the shader compares it with two uniforms. Hovering changes a uniform, **never a buffer**.

```glsl
// champion_model.gdshader (additions)
uniform int hover_region = -1;
uniform int active_region = -1;
uniform vec3 highlight : source_color = vec3(1.0, 0.85, 0.55);
varying flat int region;

void vertex() {
	region = int(round(CUSTOM0.r * 255.0));   // 255 = not editable
}

void fragment() {
	// ... the existing body ...
	if (region != 255 && region == active_region) {
		EMISSION += highlight * 0.35;
	} else if (region != 255 && region == hover_region) {
		EMISSION += highlight * 0.15;
		RIM = 0.6;
	}
}
```

`MftrModel::build_mesh` adds `ARRAY_CUSTOM0` (format `ARRAY_CUSTOM_RGBA8_UNORM`) **only for creator previews**. Game models don't carry it, and their shader branch is never taken.

### 3.3 Live bone updates without touching the mesh

While dragging, the controller writes the changed bones' **pose** on the preview's `Skeleton3D` (`set_bone_pose_position`, `set_bone_pose_rotation`, `set_bone_pose_scale`), composed as `rest · delta`.

- **Skinning runs on the GPU,** with Godot's compute skinning in Forward+ and Mobile, and vertex-shader skinning in Compatibility. Only the bone matrix buffer changes, so there's no vertex re-upload and no `ArrayMesh` rebuild.
- **The preview's idle clip never keys face bones** (validator rule), so the animator leaves them alone. The controller re-applies its deltas after the animator each frame, which costs 24 transform sets.
- **When the drag ends,** the recipe takes the face, and the next full forge (debounced, for a part or color change) bakes it into rest (§2.3).

## 4. `CharacterSculptController`

```rust
// crates/mftr-gdext/src/sculpt.rs
//! The Face step's controller (21 §4): maps the cursor to face regions, turns drags into face
//! bone deltas, poses the preview's Skeleton3D live, and hands the face back as bytes.

use godot::classes::{INode, Node, Skeleton3D};
use godot::prelude::*;
use mftr_forge::face::{self, BoneDelta, CameraFrame, DragMode, FaceSculpt, RegionTable, FACE_BONES};

#[derive(GodotClass)]
#[class(base = Node)]
pub struct CharacterSculptController {
    base: Base<Node>,
    /// The preview's skeleton (the creator sets it after each forge).
    skeleton: Option<Gd<Skeleton3D>>,
    /// Skeleton index of each face bone (-1: missing).
    bone_index: [i32; 24],
    /// Rest local transform of each face bone (the base's, before any face).
    rest: [Transform3D; 24],
    face: FaceSculpt,
    regions: Option<RegionTable>,
    limits: [face::Limits; 24],
    /// The head mesh's bind-space triangles, positions and weights (for hover raycasts on the
    /// posed head: ~2,200 triangles, skinned on the CPU only when the face changes).
    head: face::HeadProxy,
    hover: i32,
    active: i32,
    mode: DragMode,
    symmetry: bool,
    /// The world point the drag started on (the drag plane passes through it).
    anchor: Vector3,
    /// The face before this drag (undo).
    before: FaceSculpt,
}

#[godot_api]
impl INode for CharacterSculptController {
    fn init(base: Base<Node>) -> Self {
        Self {
            base,
            skeleton: None,
            bone_index: [-1; 24],
            rest: [Transform3D::IDENTITY; 24],
            face: FaceSculpt::default(),
            regions: None,
            limits: face::LIMITS,
            head: face::HeadProxy::default(),
            hover: -1,
            active: -1,
            mode: DragMode::Move,
            symmetry: true,
            anchor: Vector3::ZERO,
            before: FaceSculpt::default(),
        }
    }

    fn process(&mut self, _dt: f64) {
        // After the animator has posed the skeleton: re-apply the face (24 bones).
        self.apply_all();
    }
}

#[godot_api]
impl CharacterSculptController {
    /// The face changed (bone indices and their new deltas, 9 floats each): for listeners
    /// such as the undo panel or a network preview.
    #[signal]
    fn pose_changed(bones: PackedInt32Array, deltas: PackedFloat32Array);

    /// Bind to a freshly forged preview: its skeleton, the base's region table and head proxy
    /// (from the creator's `MftrModel`), and the recipe's face.
    #[func]
    fn bind(&mut self, skeleton: Gd<Skeleton3D>, model: Gd<crate::model::MftrModel>, face: PackedByteArray) {
        for (i, name) in FACE_BONES.iter().enumerate() {
            let idx = skeleton.find_bone(*name);
            self.bone_index[i] = idx;
            if idx >= 0 {
                self.rest[i] = skeleton.get_bone_rest(idx);
            }
        }
        let m = model.bind();
        self.regions = m.face_regions();
        self.head = m.head_proxy();
        self.face = face::from_bytes(face.as_slice()).unwrap_or_default();
        self.skeleton = Some(skeleton);
        self.head.pose(&self.face);
        self.apply_all();
    }

    /// The region under a ray (world space), or -1. Updates the hover state.
    #[func]
    fn hover(&mut self, origin: Vector3, dir: Vector3) -> i32 {
        let Some(regions) = &self.regions else { return -1 };
        let Some(model_to_world) = self.skeleton.as_ref().map(|s| s.get_global_transform()) else { return -1 };
        let inv = model_to_world.affine_inverse();
        let (o, d) = (inv * origin, inv.basis * dir);
        self.hover = match self.head.raycast([o.x, o.y, o.z], [d.x, d.y, d.z]) {
            Some(hit) => {
                self.anchor = model_to_world * Vector3::new(hit.point[0], hit.point[1], hit.point[2]);
                regions.region_of_triangle(hit.tri, hit.bary).map_or(-1, |r| r as i32)
            }
            None => -1,
        };
        self.hover
    }

    /// Start dragging the hovered region: "move", "turn", "size" or "size_width". False if
    /// nothing is under the cursor.
    #[func]
    fn begin_drag(&mut self, mode: GString, symmetry: bool) -> bool {
        if self.hover < 0 {
            return false;
        }
        self.mode = match mode.to_string().as_str() {
            "turn" => DragMode::Turn,
            "size" => DragMode::Size { width_only: false },
            "size_width" => DragMode::Size { width_only: true },
            _ => DragMode::Move,
        };
        self.symmetry = symmetry;
        self.active = self.hover;
        self.before = self.face.clone();
        true
    }

    /// A mouse movement of `relative` pixels during a drag; `camera` is creator.gd's frame.
    #[func]
    fn drag(&mut self, relative: Vector2, camera: VarDictionary) {
        let (Some(regions), Some(skeleton)) = (&self.regions, &self.skeleton) else { return };
        let Some(region) = regions.regions.get(self.active as usize).cloned() else { return };
        let cam = camera_frame(&camera);
        let a = [self.anchor.x, self.anchor.y, self.anchor.z];
        let world = face::drag_to_world(&cam, [relative.x, relative.y], a);
        let model_basis = skeleton.get_global_transform().basis;
        let mut changed = Vec::new();
        let mut targets = vec![(region.clone(), world, [relative.x, relative.y])];
        if self.symmetry && region.mirror != region.id {
            let mirror = regions.regions[region.mirror as usize].clone();
            // The other side moves the X-flipped way (in model space).
            let local = model_basis.inverse() * Vector3::new(world[0], world[1], world[2]);
            let flipped = model_basis * Vector3::new(-local.x, local.y, local.z);
            targets.push((mirror, [flipped.x, flipped.y, flipped.z], [-relative.x, relative.y]));
        }
        for (r, w, px) in targets {
            for &(bone, share) in &r.bones {
                let b = bone as usize;
                let idx = self.bone_index[b];
                if idx < 0 {
                    continue;
                }
                let rest_world = skeleton.get_global_transform() * skeleton.get_bone_global_rest(idx);
                let basis = rest_world.basis.orthonormalized();
                let axes = [basis.col_a(), basis.col_b(), basis.col_c()].map(|v| [v.x, v.y, v.z]);
                let next = face::apply_drag(self.face.bones[b], self.mode, w, px, &cam, &axes, share, &self.limits[b]);
                if next != self.face.bones[b] {
                    self.face.bones[b] = next;
                    changed.push(b);
                }
            }
        }
        if changed.is_empty() {
            return;
        }
        // All 24: a parent's new scale changes its children's compensation (cheap).
        self.apply_all();
        self.head.pose(&self.face);
        self.emit_changed(&changed);
    }

    /// Finish the drag: the face as bytes, for the recipe (one undo step per drag).
    #[func]
    fn end_drag(&mut self) -> PackedByteArray {
        self.active = -1;
        self.face_bytes()
    }

    #[func]
    fn undo(&mut self) -> PackedByteArray {
        std::mem::swap(&mut self.face, &mut self.before);
        self.apply_all();
        self.head.pose(&self.face);
        self.face_bytes()
    }

    /// Grow or shrink the hovered region by `step` (in S_UNIT log steps), mirrored if asked:
    /// the mouse wheel. One undo step per call.
    #[func]
    fn nudge_size(&mut self, step: f32, symmetry: bool) {
        let Some(regions) = &self.regions else { return };
        let Some(region) = regions.regions.get(self.hover.max(0) as usize).cloned() else { return };
        if self.hover < 0 {
            return;
        }
        let mut targets = vec![region.clone()];
        if symmetry && region.mirror != region.id {
            targets.push(regions.regions[region.mirror as usize].clone());
        }
        self.before = self.face.clone();
        let mut changed = Vec::new();
        for r in targets {
            for &(bone, share) in &r.bones {
                let b = bone as usize;
                let mut d = self.face.bones[b];
                let k = (step * share).round() as i16;
                for c in &mut d.s {
                    *c = c.saturating_add(k);
                }
                let d = face::clamp_one(d, &self.limits[b]);
                if d != self.face.bones[b] {
                    self.face.bones[b] = d;
                    changed.push(b);
                }
            }
        }
        if changed.is_empty() {
            return;
        }
        self.apply_all();
        self.head.pose(&self.face);
        self.emit_changed(&changed);
    }

    #[func]
    fn active_region(&self) -> i32 {
        self.active
    }

    #[func]
    fn face_bytes(&self) -> PackedByteArray {
        PackedByteArray::from(face::to_bytes(&self.face).as_slice())
    }
}

impl CharacterSculptController {
    /// Pose one face bone as rest · delta (the same composition `face::bake` uses).
    fn apply(&mut self, b: usize) {
        let idx = self.bone_index[b];
        let Some(skeleton) = self.skeleton.as_mut() else { return };
        if idx < 0 {
            return;
        }
        let d: BoneDelta = self.face.bones[b];
        let rest = self.rest[b];
        let [tx, ty, tz] = d.translation();
        let [qx, qy, qz, qw] = d.rotation();
        let [sx, sy, sz] = d.scale();
        // A scaled face-bone parent's scale is divided back out (as `face::bake` does).
        let inv = match face::FACE_PARENT[b] {
            Some(p) => {
                let [px, py, pz] = self.face.bones[p].scale();
                Vector3::new(1.0 / px, 1.0 / py, 1.0 / pz)
            }
            None => Vector3::ONE,
        };
        let rest_q = rest.basis.get_quaternion();
        let pos = rest.origin * inv + rest_q * Vector3::new(tx, ty, tz);
        let rot = rest_q * Quaternion::new(qx, qy, qz, qw);
        let scl = rest.basis.get_scale() * inv * Vector3::new(sx, sy, sz);
        skeleton.set_bone_pose_position(idx, pos);
        skeleton.set_bone_pose_rotation(idx, rot);
        skeleton.set_bone_pose_scale(idx, scl);
    }

    fn apply_all(&mut self) {
        for b in 0..FACE_BONES.len() {
            self.apply(b);
        }
    }

    fn emit_changed(&mut self, changed: &[usize]) {
        let bones = PackedInt32Array::from(changed.iter().map(|&b| b as i32).collect::<Vec<_>>().as_slice());
        let mut data = Vec::with_capacity(changed.len() * 9);
        for &b in changed {
            let d = self.face.bones[b];
            data.extend(d.translation());
            data.extend(d.rotation()[..3].iter().copied());
            data.extend(d.scale());
        }
        let deltas = PackedFloat32Array::from(data.as_slice());
        self.signals().pose_changed().emit(&bones, &deltas);
    }
}

fn camera_frame(d: &VarDictionary) -> CameraFrame {
    let v = |k: &str| d.get(k).and_then(|x| x.try_to::<Vector3>().ok()).unwrap_or(Vector3::ZERO);
    let f = |k: &str| d.get(k).and_then(|x| x.try_to::<f32>().ok()).unwrap_or(0.0);
    let a = |x: Vector3| [x.x, x.y, x.z];
    CameraFrame {
        origin: a(v("origin")),
        right: a(v("right")),
        up: a(v("up")),
        forward: a(v("forward")),
        fov_y: f("fov_y"),
        ortho_size: f("ortho_size"),
        viewport_h: f("viewport_h").max(1.0),
    }
}
```

Notes for whoever lands it:

- **Not compiled yet.** The code is the specification. It needs `mftr_forge::face::{LIMITS, HeadProxy}`:
  - `HeadProxy` is the head's bind-space triangles with their weights. `pose` re-skins those ~1,350 vertices on the CPU (four 4×4 matrices each, well under 0.1 ms), and `raycast` reuses `image::raycast`'s triangle test.
  - It also needs `MftrModel::face_regions()` and `head_proxy()`, read from the base's export.
- **A parent's change re-poses its children.** When a parent face bone changes, its face-bone children must be re-posed too (their scale compensation depends on it). `drag` handles this by calling `apply_all` when any changed bone has children in `FACE_PARENT`, or simply by always calling it: 24 bones is cheap.
- **The hover raycast tests the *posed* head,** so a moved nose is found where it now is.
- **`pose_changed` carries only what changed:** 9 floats per bone, at most 24 bones a drag event.

## 5. Performance and scale

- **In a match:** the face is baked into rest at load. A face therefore costs the bone count, not the face:
  - 34 biped bones + 23 new face bones (`jaw` already exists) = 57 bones a champion.
  - Ten champions skin ~12,000 vertices on the GPU, the same as today plus the new head's density.
- **In the creator,** per drag event:
  - 24 bone pose sets;
  - a ~1,350-vertex CPU skin of the hover proxy;
  - a uniform change.
  - No forge, no mesh build, no buffer upload.
- **Triangle budget** *(start, raised for the new head as the owner asked)*:
  - champion target **6,500–10,000** (was 5,000–8,000), cap **14,000** (was 12,000);
  - the head ~2,300 of it, eyes included;
  - parts and bodies keep their own counts;
  - `rules::TRIANGLES_TARGET` and `TRIANGLES_MAX` change with the head's slice (FR2).
- **Base size:** the face morph targets go, which takes ~1 MB off each humanoid base (today ~2.8 MB).
- **Shading:** at ~2,000 triangles the face's facets are smaller than the faceted style was made for. Skin already shades on a continuous ramp (2026-11-08). The head should also carry **smooth normals** (exported, used by the skin slot instead of derivative normals), so the face reads as a face and the rest of the model keeps its facets. **The owner decides this** (it changes 05 §2's faceted rule for skin).

## 6. Validation (`mftr-pack`)

A base with a face rig must have:

1. all 24 face bones, with the right parents;
2. no clip keying a face bone (`jaw` excepted, for animation);
3. at most four influences per vertex;
4. no face bone weighing on the neck ring or the two loops above it;
5. on every edge, no single bone's weight changing by more than 0.35;
6. a region for every vertex with face-bone weight, and none on the ring;
7. a mirror for every `_l` region;
8. `<base>.face.ron` matching the export.

Recipes: `face.rig` known, only known bones, every value within its limits.

### FR1 status (2026-11-09): ✅ done

**The rig standard.** `mftr_pack::face` defines `FACE_RIG`, the 24 bones and their parents, and checks a base's face rig (§6):

- every bone present with the right parent;
- no clip keys a face bone (`jaw` excepted);
- no face weight within two rings of the neck seam;
- no bone's weight changing by more than 0.35 along an edge (split vertices welded, triangulation's diagonals included).

Face vertices may use four influences without the influence warning.

**The bases.** `mftr_blender.face_rig` adds the 24 bones to the four humanoid bases (58 bones now) and weights the **block-out head**:

- a linear falloff from each bone's pivot, letting go toward the seam;
- smoothed across the mesh until no edge passes the limit;
- at most three bones a vertex, with a continuous cut so a bone fades out rather than dropping.

These are **placeholder weights**. The block-out head's edges are ~2.5 cm, so the smoothing keeps weight peaks low: a 2 cm bone move shows as ~7 mm on the head. FR2's authored head carries the real weights (§1.4).

**The bake and the skin.** `mftr_forge::face`:

- `FaceSculpt` with its limits;
- the recipe's `face:` (strict reader, writer) and the compact byte form;
- `bake`, which puts the deltas into the face bones' rest after the clips are attached, divides a scaled parent's scale out of its face-bone children, and records where each moved bone is bound in `Model::bind`;
- `skinned`, CPU skinning, which FR4's hover raycast will use.

`Rig::bind` carries the bind pose to the client, and `MftrModel::instantiate` builds the `Skin` from it rather than from the rest. **This departs from §2.3, which planned the glTF inverse bind matrices:** the forge records the bind itself, which needs no extra parsing and has the same effect.

**Checked:**

### FR3 status (2026-11-09): done

**The face sliders are presets on the bones.** `art/creator/heads/presets.txt` holds the 28 face sliders, each a set of bone moves at +1, mirrored where the slider is two-sided (`ears_point` is one-sided). The forge reads it through `mftr_forge::face::presets`, which rejects:

- an unknown bone;
- a move past a bone's limits or degrees of freedom;
- the same move given twice.

**How a recipe's sliders reach the face.** `face::compose` sums the recipe's own `face` and each face slider times its preset, rounds and clamps the result, and the forge bakes it. The sliders stay in `shape:` as written. **This departs from §2.7**, which folded them into `face` on load. Keeping them means:

- old recipes load and save byte-identical;
- a slider stays a slider in the creator;
- a retuned preset reaches every recipe that uses it.

**The bases.** The four bases dropped their face morph targets. They keep the seven body sliders as shape keys, and are now ~1.5 MB each.

**Weights, redone.** Previewing every preset at −1 and +1 showed that FR1's falloff had starved the small bones: the chin carried at most 0.05 of its vertices, the lips 0.14, the mouth corners 0.16. `face_rig.weight` now works like this:

- Each bone's falloff is a smoothstep over a tighter reach (2.5–7.5 cm).
- Overlapping bones split a vertex as falloff³, so the nearest feature wins.
- The jaw is a frame bone along the jawline, from each jaw angle to the chin, at 0.6 strength.
- Each eye takes its lids whole (a 2.2 cm core), so the eyes move inside their lids.
- The fade toward the neck counts rings from the seam, as the validator does, rather than height. The old height cut also starved the chin.

The feature bones now peak at 0.35–0.6, the eyes at 1.0. At +1 a preset moves the skin 6–15 mm, except `brow_angle` (3 mm, a turn of the brows). The steepest edge is 0.30. Pack validation is clean.

**Checks:**

- Contact sheets of every preset at −1, 0 and +1, front and ¾.
- Cairn-, Marrow- and Rook-neo's full slider mixes, composed on their own bases.
- Tests: the presets are symmetric and within limits; the presets reader is strict; sliders compose and clamp; composed faces round-trip through text and bytes; Cairn-neo's pre-rig recipe forges with its face on the bones.

- **The jaw's frame.** The jaw leans along its bone toward the chin, so a jaw move in a preset or the editor isn't "up" in the face frame; `presets.txt` says how to convert. The editor (FR4) drags in the bone's own frame, so it needs no change.

### FR4 status (2026-11-11): built, awaiting the owner's try

**Features is the Face step's default tool,** ahead of the sculpt brushes:

- Pointing at the face lights the region under the cursor and names it on the stage ("Nose tip · drag to move…").
- A drag on a region moves the feature with the cursor; a right-drag turns it.
- Shift-drag or the wheel sizes it (Alt: only across).
- With Symmetry on, the other side follows, mirrored, and lights too. On the middle line a change stays centred.
- A drag off the face turns the model; the wheel off it zooms.
- Undo (Ctrl+Z) steps back one drag, and Reset features clears them.
- The sliders stay below, summed with the drags (`face::compose`).

**Where it departs from §2.4–§4:**

- **Regions come from the weights,** not from Blender. A vertex goes to the face bone whose weight on it is the greatest share of that bone's own peak, so the 24 regions are the 24 bones, mirrored left and right. By raw weight the eyes, which carry their lids whole, would take the brows. Nothing new is exported.
- **No separate controller node.** `MftrCreator` holds the editor, since it already holds the recipe and the skinned preview: `face_hover`, `face_begin`, `face_drag`, `face_end`, `face_undo`, `face_clear`.
- **The hover raycast uses the skinned preview** (FR3's aim model), not a separate head proxy.
- **The region rides in UV2,** not `CUSTOM0`, and only on creator previews: `UV2.x = (bone + 1) / 256`, 0 for none. The shader reads it as a `flat` varying, so a triangle takes one region whole. Hover, active and mirror are uniforms; no buffer changes.
- **Live drags pose the skeleton.** Each face bone is posed as `baked_bone(bound rest, the face)`, the same function the bake now calls, so the drag shows exactly what the next forge bakes. The preview is forged once, when the drag ends.

**In `mftr_forge::face`:** `vertex_regions`, `region_at` (Möller–Trumbore over the body), `posed`, `bone_frames`, `CameraFrame`, `drag_to_world`, `DragMode`, `apply_drag`, `drag_face`, `mirror_of` and `REGION_NAMES`.

## 9. Extending to the body

The body uses the same mechanism: rest-baked bone deltas, regions, drag-to-sculpt, a fixed-point record per bone. It replaces the body morph sliders the way §2.7 replaces the face's. What the next agent needs:

### 9.1 Body sculpt bones

These are **helper bones**: leaves under the biped bones that carry mass, never keyed by clips, sculpted by scale and translation.

| Region | Helper bones (`body_*`) | Parent | Moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neck | `body_neck` | `neck` | thickness (S x, z), length (T y) |
| Shoulders | `body_trap_l/r`, `body_delt_l/r` | `clavicle_*`, `upperarm_*` | trapezius slope, deltoid mass |
| Chest | `body_pec_l/r` (or `body_bust_l/r` on feminine bases, the same bone) | `chest` | mass, height, spacing, projection |
| Belly and waist | `body_belly`, `body_waist_l/r` | `spine_01` | belly, waist width |
| Back | `body_lat_l/r` | `spine_02` | V-taper |
| Hips and glutes | `body_hip_l/r`, `body_glute_l/r` | `pelvis` | hip width, glute mass |
| Arms | `body_bicep_l/r`, `body_forearm_l/r` | `upperarm_*`, `forearm_*` | arm mass (S), muscle peak (T) |
| Legs | `body_thigh_l/r`, `body_calf_l/r` | `thigh_*`, `calf_*` | leg mass, calf shape |

That is about 28 bones. With the biped's 34 and the face's 23, a champion has ~85, well inside Godot's limits and the GPU skinning budget.

### 9.2 What changes

- **Proportions stay bone lengths** (today's `Proportions`: height, legs, arms, torso, head, hands). Helper deltas are relative to the proportioned rest, so the two compose: legs get longer *and* thicker.
- **Weights:**
  - The body mesh is re-weighted so each region's mass sits on its helper with the same core-and-falloff rule as the face (§1.4), with a ≤ 0.35 per-edge change.
  - The biped bones keep what bends at joints.
  - Helpers stay off the joint creases: elbow, knee, shoulder pit, hip crease. A helper scaled under a bending joint would fight the bend.
- **Scale never cascades:** every helper is a leaf.
- **Parts follow by weights:**
  - Each part's weights are transferred from the nearest body surface, helpers included, so a breastplate grows with the chest and a boot with the calf. This fixes report 8 (§8) properly.
  - Rigid parts (plates, buckles) take the helper's translation and only part of its scale: a `rigid` flag in the part's catalog entry caps the scale they inherit at ±10%.
- **Animation:**
  - Clips never key helpers.
  - Retargeting (`mftr_forge::Retarget`) passes them through untouched.
  - Grip and foot IK use the proportioned skeleton, which helpers don't change.
  - Helpers sit at the surface, so mass doesn't shift a hand's reach.
- **Hitboxes stay honest:** the sim's radius is the champion's, not the mesh's, and helper limits keep the silhouette within the budget 20 §6.2 sets.
- **Data:**
  - `looks.body: (rig: 1, bones: (body_pec_l: (s: (120, 80, 120)), …))`, the same `BoneDelta` and the same binary form (another 32-bit mask).
  - `face` and `body` can share one record with a 64-bit mask if that's simpler for the wire.
- **Editor:**
  - The Body step gets the same hover and drag on the body's regions.
  - Proportions stay as sliders (they are lengths, best set by number).
  - The body morph sliders become presets on the helpers (as §2.7).
- **Performance:** baked into rest like the face. A crowd of sculpted bodies costs exactly ordinary skinning.

### 9.3 Order

The face proves the mechanism (FR1–FR4). The body then needs only bones, weights and regions. In order:

1. Helper bones and re-weighting of one base, with parts' weight transfer.
2. The Body step's drag.
3. The other three bases.
4. Body slider migration.
